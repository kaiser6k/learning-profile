# Plans — IEP, 504, college

Read this before any answer about school rights, a plan, or an accommodation note.

## Split

| | IEP | Section 504 plan | College access |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Law | IDEA | Rehabilitation Act of 1973, Subpart D | Section 504 Subpart E, and the ADA |
| Who | K–12. Ages about 3–21, or until high-school graduation. | K–12 student whose impairment substantially limits a major life activity. | Student who meets entry rules and documents a disability. |
| Needs | Special instruction. One of 13 IDEA categories. | Access in the regular class. No special instruction required. | Reasonable access. Not a guarantee of success. |
| Document | Written IEP. Team includes parents. | 504 plan. Team includes parents. A student does not hold both at once. IEP covers it. | Accommodation letter from disability services. Student negotiates. |
| Can change the work | Yes, if the team writes a modification. | Rarely. Same curriculum. | No. No shorter tests, fewer questions, or a new grade rule as the default. |
| Follows the student | Ends at graduation. Does not transfer. | Does not transfer to college. | Starts only after the student registers. |

Major life activities include learning, reading, concentrating, thinking, and communicating. Look at the limit without medicine or tools propping it up.

## Supports to offer, by barrier

Same work unless they ask to shrink it.

| Barrier | Put on the plan or note |
| --- | --- |
| Reading | Text-to-speech, audiobooks, questions read aloud, oral and written directions |
| Writing | Speech-to-text, type instead of handwrite, scribe, sentence starters, copy of notes, partial outline |
| Numbers | Calculator when the skill is not computation, fact chart, graph paper, formula sheet, story problems in steps |
| ADHD | Written directions, chunked work, short quizzes, a break in a long task, extra time, quiet room |

K–12 only, and only if they ask: fewer problems at the same level. That is still access if the level stays. A shorter easier text is a modification. Label it.

## What not to draft

- A signed IEP, 504, or doctor letter.
- A diagnosis they did not state.
- A claim that a high-school plan is active in college.
- A claim that a college has no duty at all. It has a duty not to discriminate, after the student asks.
